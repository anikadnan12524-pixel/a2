Clothing care made simple

Washing, drying, stain removal, storage and easy repairs: the everyday habits that keep clothes looking good for longer.

01Washing

Wash less, wash cooler

Most everyday clothes come out just as clean at 30°C (86°F) as at 40°C, and washing cooler is gentler on colours, elastic and fibres. Sort by colour and weight, close zips and buttons, and turn printed, dark and textured pieces inside out.

  • Don’t overload the machine: clothes need room to move to get clean.
  • Use less detergent than you think; too much leaves residue.
  • Use a mesh bag for delicates, bras and small items.
  • Air between wears: hang knitwear and jackets overnight instead of washing them.
02Stains

Quick stain guide

Act fast, blot rather than rub and always check a stain is gone before drying, as heat can set it permanently.

StainWhat to do
GrassPre-treat with a little liquid detergent, rub gently and wash as usual at the hottest temperature the label allows.
Coffee or teaRinse from the back with cold water straight away, then dab with detergent before washing.
Grease and oilSprinkle with cornflour or baking soda to absorb, brush off, then work in washing-up liquid and wash.
InkDab (don’t rub) with a little isopropyl rubbing spirit on a cotton pad, working from the outside in; test on a hidden area first.
Sweat marksSoak in cool water with a little oxygen-based stain remover before washing; avoid hot water, which can set them.
MudLet it dry completely, brush off as much as possible, then pre-treat and wash.
03Storage

Store clothes the right way

Fold knits

Hanging stretches sweaters out of shape. Fold them and stack the heaviest at the bottom.

Hang structure

Shirts, jackets, coats and dresses keep their shape on wide, sturdy hangers.

Let them breathe

Avoid packing rails too tightly and store off-season clothes clean, in breathable bags.

Deter moths

Wash or brush wool before storing and use cedar blocks or lavender sachets.

Rest your shoes

Give leather shoes a day off between wears and use shoe trees to keep their shape.

Keep it dry

Damp causes mildew; never put away anything that isn’t completely dry.

04Repairs

Easy repairs anyone can do

  • Sew on a button with a double thread and a few loops around the shank.
  • Fix a dropped hem with a slip stitch or iron-on hemming tape.
  • De-pill knitwear with a fabric comb or electric shaver, working gently.
  • Mend small holes early, before they grow, with a simple darn.
  • Know when to call a tailor for zips, linings and resizing.
