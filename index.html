        House rules

        Eight rules for a wardrobe that works

        Fit beats label

        A simple piece that fits well looks better than an expensive one that doesn’t.

        Start neutral

        Build on two or three base colours, then add accents you love.

        Dress your real life

        Buy for the days you actually have, not the ones you imagine.

        Feel the fabric

        Natural and well-made fibres wear, breathe and age better.

        The 30-wear test

        Before buying, ask if you’ll wear it at least 30 times.

        One in, one out

        Keep your wardrobe the same size as it slowly improves.

        Care is style

        Pressed, clean and repaired clothes always look intentional.

        Tailor the basics

        A quick hem or nip at the waist transforms everyday pieces.

        The ten-minute reset

        Edit your wardrobe this weekend

        You don’t need a full clear-out to feel the difference. Give yourself an hour and work through one section at a time.

        1. Take everything out of one drawer or one section of the rail.
        2. Sort into three piles: love and wear, needs fixing, and no longer fits your life.
        3. Try on anything unsure. If it doesn’t fit or feel good, let it go.
        4. Book the repairs and donate or recycle the rest responsibly.
        5. Put back only what earns its place, grouped by type and colour.

        Questions

        Everyday wardrobe FAQ

        Straight answers to the questions readers ask us most about clothes, capsules and care.

        What is a capsule wardrobe?

        A capsule wardrobe is a small collection of versatile clothes that all work together, so almost any top can be worn with almost any bottom. Most people aim for 25 to 40 pieces per season, including shoes and outer layers.

        How do I read a clothing care label?

        Care labels use five basic symbols: a tub for washing, a triangle for bleaching, a square for drying, an iron for ironing and a circle for professional cleaning. Dots show temperature, and a cross through any symbol means don’t.

        How often should I wash my clothes?

        Underwear, socks and workout clothes need washing after every wear. T-shirts and shirts usually after one or two wears, jeans after several, and knitwear and coats only when they look or smell like they need it. Airing clothes between wears goes a long way.

        How can I make my clothes last longer?

        Wash less often and at lower temperatures, turn clothes inside out, close zips, air-dry where possible, fold knits instead of hanging them and fix small problems like loose buttons straight away.

        What should I buy first when rebuilding a wardrobe?

        Start with the pieces you wear most often in your daily life: well-fitting trousers or jeans, a few plain tops, a layer such as a cardigan or jacket, and comfortable shoes in a neutral colour. Add accent pieces once the basics work together.
