The everyday wardrobe guide

How to build a small, hard-working wardrobe: the essential pieces, how clothes should fit, choosing colours and shopping more thoughtfully.

01The essentials

Start with the pieces you wear most

A good everyday wardrobe is built from the bottom up. Begin with the clothes that do the heavy lifting in your week, then add a few pieces with personality. These essentials suit most lifestyles; adjust the numbers for your climate and routine.

Tops

Plain T-shirts, a couple of button-up shirts and fine knits in your base colours.

Bottoms

Well-fitting jeans, tailored trousers and a skirt or chinos that suit your days.

Layers

A cardigan or sweatshirt, a smart jacket and one dependable coat.

Dresses and one-pieces

One easy dress or jumpsuit that works dressed up or down, if they suit you.

Shoes

Clean trainers, a smarter pair and something weatherproof.

Finishing touches

A belt, a scarf and a bag that goes with everything.

02Fit

How clothes should fit

Fit makes more difference than price, brand or trend. Use these checkpoints when trying anything on, and remember that a tailor can fix sleeve and trouser lengths cheaply.

CheckWhat to look for
ShouldersThe seam should sit at the edge of your shoulder bone, not past it or pulling inwards.
SleevesShirt and jacket sleeves end at the wrist bone; a little shirt cuff can show under a jacket.
Chest and buttonsButtons lie flat with no pulling into an X shape when you stand or sit.
WaistTrousers stay up without a belt and you can fit two fingers inside the waistband.
LengthTops cover the waistband when you lift your arms; trousers just touch the top of your shoes.
MovementSit, reach and walk in the fitting room. Comfort is part of a good fit.
03Colour

A simple colour plan

The easiest wardrobes are built on a short colour list. Pick two or three base neutrals that suit you, such as navy, grey, black, camel or olive, then one or two accent colours you love. Almost everything will then go with everything else.

  • Bases (about 70%): trousers, coats, knitwear and shoes.
  • Lights (about 20%): white, cream or pale blue shirts and tees.
  • Accents (about 10%): a coloured knit, a patterned scarf or a bright top.
04Shopping

Shop slower, choose better

  • Keep a short wish list and wait a week before buying anything that isn’t on it.
  • Check the fabric label and the seams: tight, even stitching is a good sign.
  • Think in outfits: name three things you already own that it will go with.
  • Look second-hand first for coats, knitwear and denim, which often last for years.
  • Spend more where you wear most: shoes and coats get the hardest use.
